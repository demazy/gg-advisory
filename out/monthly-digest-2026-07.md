## Energy Transition

**AEMC Final Rule Enhances Grid Visibility**
Published: 16 July 2026  
Summary: The Australian Energy Market Commission (AEMC) has introduced a rule to improve visibility and planning of local electricity grids, driven by consumer energy resources like rooftop solar and EVs. Distribution Network Service Providers must now publish a 20-year Distribution Network Development Plan. This rule is part of a broader reform to integrate consumer energy resources into the energy system.  
Why it matters: Boards of energy companies should align their strategic planning with the new 20-year horizon requirement to ensure compliance and optimize grid investments.  
Signals to watch: Monitor the Australian Energy Regulator's development of detailed data guidelines.  
Source: [AEMC News](https://www.aemc.gov.au/news-centre/media-releases/new-rule-make-invisible-visible-across-distribution-network)

**Solar PV Installer Banned for Non-compliance**
Published: 13 July 2026  
Summary: The Clean Energy Regulator has banned Sandeep Laxman Shinde from installing solar PV systems under the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme for three years due to non-compliance, including failing to meet onsite attendance requirements and providing false statements.  
Why it matters: Solar companies must ensure compliance with accreditation requirements to avoid similar penalties and maintain eligibility for small-scale technology certificates.  
Signals to watch: Track updates on compliance requirements from the Clean Energy Regulator.  
Source: [CER News](https://www.cer.gov.au/news-and-media/news/2026/july/solar-pv-installer-declared-ineligible-to-install-solar-systems-and-batteries-under-sres-sandeep)

## ESG Reporting

**EFRAG Launches Consultation on ESRS-40a Draft**
Published: 23 July 2026  
Summary: EFRAG has opened a 100-day public consultation on the ESRS-40a Exposure Draft, targeting non-EU undertakings with significant EU activities. The draft supports the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive and aims to ensure transparency on environmental impacts. Feedback will shape EFRAG's final advice to the European Commission, expected in January 2027.  
Why it matters: Australian companies with EU operations should prepare to comply with ESRS-40a standards by 2028, ensuring transparency in sustainability impacts.  
Signals to watch: EFRAG's final advice to the European Commission in January 2027.  
Source: [EFRAG News](https://www.efrag.org/en/news-and-calendar/news/efrag-launches-public-consultation-on-the-esrs40a-exposure-draft-for-certain-noneu-undertakings)

**APRA Ends Alternate Submission Process for ADIs**
Published: 31 July 2026  
Summary: APRA has announced that from 31 July 2026, all ADIs must submit reporting collections via APRA Connect, ending the Alternate Submission Process. This change aims to improve data accuracy and consistency.  
Why it matters: CFOs at ADIs must ensure their teams are ready to use APRA Connect for submissions starting 31 July 2026 to avoid compliance issues.  
Signals to watch: APRA's updates on any further changes to reporting processes.  
Source: [APRA News](https://www.apra.gov.au/news-and-publications/end-alternate-submission-process-adis-and-rfcs)

## Cleantech & Start-up Ecosystem — Grants & Accelerators Radar

**National programs — open on a rolling basis**

**Australia's Economic Accelerator (AEA) — Ignite & Innovate**
Administrator: Department of Education / Australian Government
Funding: Ignite: up to $500,000 (12 months); Innovate: up to $5,000,000 (proof-of-scale)
Deadline: Rolling rounds — monitor aea.gov.au for next opening
Stage: University research teams with industry co-applicant (Ignite: proof-of-concept; Innovate: proof-of-scale)
Summary: Australia's $1.6 billion Economic Accelerator funds university research commercialisation through two streams. Ignite grants (up to $500K, 12 months) support proof-of-concept and laboratory testing; Innovate grants (up to $5M) advance technologies from lab to proof-of-scale via industry-university collaboration. Round 1 allocated over $130M across 194 projects. Climate-tech, energy, and materials ventures regularly feature. Requires a university lead applicant with an industry co-applicant.
Why it matters: The primary pathway for deep-tech climate startups spun out of Australian universities to access non-dilutive federal commercialisation funding. Investors and founders with university IP should engage their tech-transfer office about a joint AEA application.
Signals to watch: Round 2 details and opening dates are published on aea.gov.au. The $1.6B envelope means multiple rounds are expected across 2026–2030.
Sources: https://www.aea.gov.au/researcher-applicant/grants

**ARENA Advancing Renewables Program**
Administrator: Australian Renewable Energy Agency (ARENA)
Funding: Flexible, no fixed cap per project
Deadline: Always open
Stage: Development, demonstration, pre-commercial deployment (TRL 4+)
Summary: ARENA's foundational program funds development, demonstration, and pre-commercial deployment across all renewable energy and grid technologies. No fixed cap per project; milestone-based disbursement. Submit an EOI in ARENANet — ARENA staff recommend calling before submitting. **R&D Tax Incentive (R&DTI)**
Administrator: ATO + Department of Industry, Science and Resources (DISR)
Funding: 43.5% refundable tax offset (SMEs, turnover < $20M); 38.5%+ non-refundable (larger companies)
Deadline: Register within 10 months of financial year end (e.g. 30 April 2026 for FY25)
Stage: Any incorporated Australian company conducting eligible R&D activities
Summary: Australia's largest innovation incentive offers a 43.5% refundable tax offset on eligible R&D expenditure for SMEs with turnover under $20M — effectively a cash rebate on R&D costs. Eligible costs include wages for R&D staff, contractor costs, and materials used in trials. Larger companies receive a non-refundable offset based on R&D intensity. Register R&D activities with DISR (via business.gov.au) before claiming in your tax return; registration must be lodged within 10 months of financial year end. The eligible expenditure cap was increased to $150M in recent amendments.
Why it matters: For any climate-tech startup conducting genuine technical experimentation — hardware prototypes, novel software, new materials processes — this is the single most accessible cash injection available. It's not a competitive grant; all eligible companies receive the offset.
Signals to watch: Consult an R&D tax specialist before lodging — the ATO audits R&D claims closely and requires contemporaneous records of technical uncertainty, hypothesis, experiments, and outcomes.
Sources: https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/research-and-development-tax-incentive
